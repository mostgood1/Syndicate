### 1. THE WORKTREE REGISTRY WAS CARRYING 36 ENTRIES NOTHING COULD SEE

- **VERIFIED, and the asymmetry is the finding:** a registration whose
  `.git/worktrees/<name>/gitdir` file is missing is **hidden from
  `git worktree list`** while still occupying `.git/worktrees/`. `list` reported
  **83** while the directory held **118**. That is why 36 dead entries
  accumulated with nobody noticing — the instrument everyone reads does not show
  them.
- **VERIFIED cause, and my first diagnosis was WRONG.** I said "ReadOnly on the
  directories is exactly why deletion fails". Windows largely IGNORES `ReadOnly`
  on directories; it honours it on FILES. The real blockers were the `logs` and
  `refs` files inside each `.git/worktrees/<name>`.
- **VERIFIED that the remedy I proposed does NOT work.** `attrib -R /S /D` left
  the count unchanged — **118 ReadOnly before, 118 after**. What works is
  `Remove-Item -Recurse -Force`, because `-Force` overrides `ReadOnly` itself.
  I only learned this because I ran the remedy and measured it.
- **Result, all verified after:** metadata dirs **118 → 83**, dead registrations
  **36 → 0**, husk directories **16 → 0**, three of my own scratch worktrees
  removed, `prunable 0`, `locks 0`, every listed worktree present on disk. Live
  worktrees re-checked afterwards: `ncaaf-live-resim-wire` → `e2a36a14`,
  `edge-basis-moneyline` → `20288681`.
- **`f97ad5ab-close`: I asserted intent from a flag and was wrong.** I said the
  lock was "a deliberate act by whoever created it, so not mine to clear". The
  reason file said `initializing` — git's OWN automatic lock during
