# Session (cont.) — engine: the goal line did not end drives

## VERIFIED

**`PlayOutcome.TOUCHDOWN` is SAMPLED from a weight distribution and was the ONLY
way a drive could score six.** An ordinary GAIN/EXPLOSIVE_GAIN carried its full
yardage regardless of field remaining, while `advance_field_position` clamps the
yardline to [1, 100] — so the ball pinned itself ON the goal line and the drive
kept running plays that gained yards going nowhere until the quarter expired.

    2,409 NCAAF drives          BEFORE      AFTER (same seeds)
    yards_gained > 100           6.60%       0.00%
    gained >75, scored NOTHING   3.82%       0.07%
    longest single drive        249 yd      99 yd

**This was the drive-structure gap.** NCAAF primary error vs the truth report
13.04% -> 5.20% from the fix alone; 3.94% with `drive_yardage_multiplier`
re-fitted 1.15 -> 0.95. possessions/game -15.3% -> -2.6%, plays/drive
+25.6% -> +9.1%, seconds/drive +10.6% -> -3.5%.

**IT SHIPS OFF, and that is the finding, not a hedge.** Shared Football Core
code, so this was NFL's bug too — and NFL, which is production, gets WORSE:

    NFL mean |err| vs truth   4.8% -> 5.9%
    NFL touchdown rate       +3.5% -> +17.2%
    NFL game total           -3.5% -> +10.6%

Both profiles were calibrated WITH the defect, so fixing the mechanism
invalidates the estimators absorbing it. A one-parameter re-fit does not
recover NFL: `drive_yardage_multiplier` 1.00 -> 0.92 trades touchdowns and game
total for possessions and punt rate, landing back at 5.9%. NCAAF's re-fit has
its own costs — yards/drive +1.5% -> -21.7%, field-goal rate untouched at +74%.

Gated on `SYNDICATE_FOOTBALL_GOAL_LINE_TOUCHDOWN`, absent reads OFF.
**No profile values changed.** 6 tests, 44 green across the engine suites.

## THREE BELIEFS I HELD THIS SESSION THAT THE DATA OVERTURNED

Recorded because the sequence matters more than any one of them:

1. **"The engine runs 18% too fast on pace."** Wrong — I quoted
   `drive_priors.expected_clock_seconds` (151.6s), a PRIOR, as though it were
   the simulation's realized output (183.0s). It runs too SLOW.
2. **"yards/play is 18.9% below truth."** Wrong — that was DERIVED as
   drive_yards / drive_plays. Measured the way the truth report measures it,
   +0.7%. In real football the two are identical by construction; comparing a
   derived quantity to a measured one manufactured a defect.
3. **"Feed the real-world pace measurement."** Wrong — the engine's
   `pace_seconds_per_play` is not on the real-world scale its name implies.
   Feeding the true league mean moves every truth metric further from target.

Each was stated with confidence and each was wrong for the same reason: a
number was taken from the nearest available field rather than from the
quantity the claim was about.
